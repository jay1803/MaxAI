# Planning

Planning runs from issue templates: a weekly plan every Monday, a daily plan
on request or each morning, and a quarterly review. Planning issues are
requests, not work items: they get no priority and no due date and are left
out of counts.

## What the planner may do

Without asking: set priority, set or move planned due dates, add the
`external ETA` label when a source states the date, and create sub-issues when
asked for a breakdown. It must not change states, delete issues, write to the
calendar or contact anyone; it proposes those with evidence.

## Rules

- **Priority** follows a short, explicit order of workstreams for the quarter,
  set by the owner. Anything overdue or due within three days is urgent.
- **External dates never move.** If one cannot be met, the planner says so.
- **Planned dates are the planner's**, and an issue without a date is
  invisible in the date-based views, so focus items get one.
- **Capacity**: plan about 70% of free hours, after reading meetings from the
  calendar (read only). Three to five focus items plus quick wins.
- An item whose planned date moved three times is flagged; the owner decides
  to split, downgrade or drop it.

## Weekly plan

1. Collect last week: completed, created, updated but still open, project
   updates.
2. Collect the current state: open, overdue, due in seven days, waiting,
   untouched for 14 days, finished but not closed.
3. Read this week's meetings and free hours.
4. Choose the focus: external dates in the next two weeks, then urgent, then
   the top workstream, then items the owner named as key focus.
5. Write planned dates, fix priority drift, read back.
6. Reply in the session: last week by workstream; this week's focus with the
   reason for each; what was changed; what the owner must decide.

## Daily plan

Yesterday (done, not done and why), today (three to five items in order,
meeting hours and free hours), dates moved, and decisions needed. Earlier
daily plan issues are closed.

## Quarterly review: fire yourself

The question: "If I were replaced tomorrow, what would my successor obviously
do in the first weeks, and why am I not doing it?" It surfaces the work that
keeps losing to more comfortable work.

1. Collect evidence: stalled top-priority issues, long waits, decisions
   deferred for weeks, dates moved repeatedly, work that consumes time with no
   result.
2. Write the successor's list: three to five actions, each tied to evidence.
3. For each, name why it has not happened and the smallest first step.
